The Quiet Tins

The wise old shopkeeper. Has watched two years of PSX and doesn't get excited easily.

Like you're five: imagine a shelf of tins. Shake them. The empty ones rattle loudly; the full ones barely make a sound. The Quiet Tins strategy ignores the rattling and picks the heavy, calm tins that have been sitting near the top shelf.

How it picks, step by step

  1. Only shops in busy aislesIt looks at the 60 most-traded stocks, so we can always buy and sell easily.
  2. Gives each stock five marks out of 100Is it calm (small daily wobbles)? Is it near its best price of the year? Do lots of people trade it? Did it avoid a big drop recently? Has it been slowly climbing for a month?
  3. Averages the marksEvery mark counts equally. No favourites.
  4. Throws out the show-offsAnything that jumped more than 6% yesterday is out. After a huge market party, anything too stretched is out too.
  5. Picks the top fiveNo more than two from the same industry, then holds them for one week.

Goldfish

The fresh-faced intern. Forgot everything before our first day, 10 November 2026, but remembers every week since.

Like you're five: a goldfish wakes up in a new bowl with no memories. Every week it watches which stocks won, writes down what they had in common, and next week it hunts for stocks that look like those winners. Its notebook only gets thicker; it never tears out a page.

How it picks, step by step

  1. Watches one week happenIt waits for each 5-day window to finish.
  2. Plays detectiveFor 8 simple clues (how much a stock jumped yesterday, this week, this month, how stretched or loud it is, how its industry did) it asks: did this clue point at winners or losers?
  3. Writes it in the notebookClues that pointed at winners get a plus; clues that pointed at losers get a minus.
  4. Averages its whole notebookEvery week since day one counts equally.
  5. Picks the top five look-alikesMax two per industry, hold for a week, then learn again.

The Great November Faceplant

On 10 November our first formula picked five stocks. By 13 November all five had fallen. Press the button to find out why.

$ waiting for a brave founder to press the button
Case closed

Cause of death: we bought the loudest tins right after the party peaked.

  • We arrived at 3 a.m. The market had already jumped 8.6% in five days. We showed up as the music was stopping and ordered five more drinks.
  • We picked the shoutiest guests. LUCK, BOP, NBP, DGKC and ENGROH had all just leapt on huge volume. Very loud. Mostly air.
  • Our formula had never been tested. It was a confident guess in a nice suit. When we finally tested it on two years of data, it was about as good as flipping a coin.
  • Meanwhile, the quiet tins won. Energy stocks that sat out the rally (MARI +8%, PSO, ATRL, POL) quietly went up while our stars fell.
  • The bill: our five picks −2.8% in three days, the KSE-100 only −0.8%.

The founder's rules of the shelf

Everything above follows from these. They don't change when the market does.
